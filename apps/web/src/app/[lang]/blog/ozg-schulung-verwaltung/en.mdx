Germany's Online Access Act (OZG) requires federal, state, and municipal government to offer administrative services online. The OZG 2.0 amendment has been in force since July 2024, its technical requirements are being rolled out starting 2026, and municipalities must implement them as mandatory starting 2028. For administrative staff, that means new digital procedures are being introduced regardless of whether structured training for them exists or not.

## Why OZG rollout often fails without training

Digitizing a new administrative procedure doesn't solve the actual problem if staff can't confidently operate the new process. In practice, this leads staff to fall back on the old, paper-based way whenever they're unsure, even if the new digital procedure has long been available. The investment in digitization then partly goes to waste, because the rollout wasn't accompanied by enabling staff to actually use it.

## What OZG training should cover

| Component                       | Goal                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Why the process is changing     | Brief context on the OZG obligation, so the change doesn't feel like an end in itself |
| The new procedure, step by step | Concrete operation, not just an announcement                                          |
| Handling exceptions             | What to do when a case doesn't fit the new procedure                                  |
| Where to find help              | Point of contact or quick reference for day-to-day work                               |

## Practical challenges in implementation

Municipalities face particular conditions when planning training that private companies rarely have in this form:

- **Cross-department standardization:** A single procedure often affects multiple departments at once, with different levels of prior knowledge and different digital habits.
- **Skills shortage and retirement wave:** New and career-changing employees need to be onboarded in parallel with the ongoing digitization, not after it.
- **Procurement law:** Training offerings need to fit the procurement reality of the given municipality; larger tenders take correspondingly longer than in the private sector.

## Frequently asked questions

### From when do municipalities need to implement OZG 2.0?

The technical requirements from the amendment are being rolled out starting 2026; municipalities must implement them as mandatory starting 2028. The specific design and sequencing of individual procedures varies by state and municipality.

### Does every administrative employee need to be trained separately on every procedure?

Not necessarily every person for every procedure; what makes sense is training those who work with the given procedure day to day, concretely and practically, rather than offering a general digitization course for everyone.

### Is a written instruction sheet enough instead of training?

A plain PDF instruction sheet is often not read in practice, or can't be found again the moment a case deviates from the standard scenario. A short, interactive training with a knowledge check increases the likelihood that the new process is actually applied correctly.

### What does OZG have to do with data protection?

OZG digitization and data protection requirements are two separate but often simultaneously relevant topics, because new digital procedures frequently process personal data. Accompanying data protection training is therefore often sensible, but it isn't part of the OZG obligation itself.
