Gemini Notebook (known as NotebookLM until July 2026), ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude can all be used for free to learn, but they differ significantly in what they're best suited for. One thing worth clarifying up front: despite now sharing part of a name, Gemini Notebook and Gemini are two different Google products. Gemini Notebook is strong when your own documents should be the foundation. ChatGPT and Claude are well suited to open-ended explanations and follow-up questions on a topic, independent of a specific source. Gemini (the general chatbot) is tightly integrated with Google Search and works well for fast, current information.

## The four tools at a glance

| Tool                                            | Strength for learning                                                                    | Limitation                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)           | Answers exclusively from your own uploaded sources, well protected against hallucination | The result stays a chat window, not a reusable course           |
| ChatGPT                                         | Broad, flexible explanations on almost any topic, good conversational flow               | Answers aren't always anchored to a specific source of your own |
| Claude                                          | Detailed, well-structured explanations, strong at processing long documents              | Like ChatGPT, primarily a chat, not a structured course format  |
| Gemini (chatbot, separate from Gemini Notebook) | Good integration with Google services, current information from search                   | Answer quality varies more by topic than with specialized tools |

## What each tool is best suited for

If you want to work through and understand an existing document, such as a manual or a study, Gemini Notebook usually gets you there fastest, because its answers stay strictly grounded in the uploaded sources. If instead you want to understand a topic from scratch without a specific source of your own at hand, ChatGPT or Claude often get you further, since both can explain more broadly. For current, fast-changing topics, the Gemini chatbot's search integration often delivers more up-to-date information.

## What all four tools are missing for actual learning

All four are primarily chat tools: a question gets asked, an answer comes back, and then the session is over. That's enough for individual lookups. For structured learning across a team, though, exactly what a course provides is missing: a fixed sequence, moments of repetition after a few days, and a knowledge check that shows whether the content was actually understood, not just read once. If you want to turn a chat result into a reusable training format for several colleagues, you need an extra step from chat answer to structured course.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which AI tool is best for a company's own documents?

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is specifically designed to generate answers exclusively from uploaded sources, which makes it especially well suited for working with a company's own documents. For sensitive or personal company data, it's worth checking the privacy angle first, see [Gemini Notebook in the Enterprise: Where the GDPR Line Sits](/blog/gemini-notebook-unternehmen-dsgvo).

### Is Gemini Notebook the same as the Gemini chatbot?

No, despite sharing part of a name, these are two different products. Gemini Notebook specializes in working with your own uploaded sources, while the Gemini chatbot is a general conversation and search tool without that source binding.

### Are ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini suitable for company training?

For individual understanding and follow-up questions, all three work well. For structured, reusable training across an entire team, they lack the course structure that goes beyond a single chat conversation.

### Is any one of the four tools fundamentally better than the others?

No, they're each stronger for different use cases. The choice depends on whether your own source should be the foundation, how current the information needs to be, and how detailed the explanation should be.

### How do you turn a good AI answer into a course for the whole team?

The key step is converting a one-off answer into a reusable format with structure, a learning objective, and a knowledge check, instead of having every person ask the same question individually.
