Many mid-sized companies have already rolled out AI tools like Copilot or ChatGPT, but without structured training on them. The result: employees use the tools with wildly varying skill, some not at all, others with no sense of what information they're even allowed to enter. AI training closes exactly this gap between "the tool is available" and "the team actually uses it competently and safely."

## Why rolling out the tool alone isn't enough

A company that enables Copilot or ChatGPT hasn't thereby built AI competence within the team. Without training, a very uneven picture usually emerges: a few employees experiment a lot and use the tools productively, while most stick to cautious or no usage at all, out of uncertainty or lack of a reason to start. For leadership, that's an uncomfortable moment: money was invested, but the benefit is hard to demonstrate.

## What good baseline AI training covers

| Component                   | Content                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fundamentals                | What AI tools can and can't do, without overstating in either direction      |
| Safe use                    | What information may be entered, what may not (customer data, trade secrets) |
| Prompting basics            | How to phrase a request so the result is usable                              |
| Company-specific guidelines | Concrete rules from the company itself, not just general tips                |

## The trigger is often a specific moment

The decision to run structured AI training rarely happens proactively; it usually comes at a specific point: a question from leadership about how it's ensured that teams use the tools correctly, or a data protection incident where unclear input data became an issue. Waiting for that moment means training reactively instead of preventively. A short, company-specific training built from your own existing AI policy can be set up within a few days, see also the [AI Usage Policy Template](/blog/ki-richtlinie-unternehmen-vorlage).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should sensible baseline AI training take?

For a solid foundation, a few short units of 10 to 15 minutes each are usually enough, covering fundamentals, safe use, and company-specific rules. A single long in-person session is rarely necessary and harder to scale than several short e-learning units.

### Is a written AI policy enough without accompanying training?

A policy alone is often not read, or is quickly forgotten. A short, interactive training that delivers the same content and ends with a knowledge check significantly increases the likelihood that the rules are actually applied day to day.

### Does every department need to go through the same AI training?

A shared baseline for everyone makes sense, supplemented with deeper content for higher-risk roles, such as people using AI for customer communication or decision preparation.

### How does AI training relate to the EU AI Act obligation?

Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires companies that deploy AI systems to ensure an adequate level of AI competence among the employees involved. Structured baseline training is an obvious building block for meeting that, see [EU AI Act: The AI Literacy Obligation Explained](/blog/eu-ai-act-ki-kompetenz-pflicht).
